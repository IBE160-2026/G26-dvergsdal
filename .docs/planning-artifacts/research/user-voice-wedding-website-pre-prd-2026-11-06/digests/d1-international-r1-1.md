# D1a — International wedding/RSVP products

Scope: Joy (withjoy.com), Zola, The Knot, RSVPify, Greenvelope. Minted, Paperless Post and WedSites were not researched because the tool-call budget ran out (see "Leads not chased").
Method: about 20 tool calls, two rounds. Official help-center pages were preferred. Where a claim rests only on a search-result snippet and the page was not fetched, confidence is marked medium or low.

## Patterns worth borrowing (your synthesis, tied to findings above)
1. **The party is the unit of response, the guest is the unit of data.** Joy, Zola and Greenvelope all let one person answer for the party, but attendance and meal choice are stored per guest (Q1, Q2). For this wedding, the household gets one personal link, and each person in it gets per-event attendance and meal rows.
2. **A personal link instead of name search.** Name search is the biggest source of complaints, through exact-match failures and lockouts (Zola, WeddingWire threads). A per-household tokenized link, close to Greenvelope's model, avoids it completely. Keep a fallback for guests who lose the link, for example asking the couple to resend it.
3. **One schedule, visibility per event.** Joy's "Specific guests" / tags model (Q2, Q6): guests see only the events they are invited to, each event has its own questions, and there is one adaptive RSVP flow. Events that need no answer stay on the schedule as information only.
4. **Conditional follow-up questions.** Ask about meals and allergies only for events a person attends (Joy follow-up questions). Use "Ask once per household" for questions such as arrival time or cabin preferences (Joy "Ask Once Per: Household").
5. **A deadline with a grace period and a closing message.** Joy's deadline features are worth copying: the date shown next to the button, a configurable grace period, and a custom message after the deadline (Q3). Decide explicitly whether answers lock for guests while the couple can still edit; Joy leaves this unclear.
6. **Admin totals by event and meal, plus CSV export.** Zola has meal and adult/child breakdowns, and Greenvelope exports everything to CSV (Q4). For this site that means totals per event per meal, a list of non-responders, and a CSV for the venue or caterer.
7. **Edit until the deadline, with a disabled Submit until something changes** (Joy Q3). This is a small UX detail that prevents empty resubmits.
8. **Page-level password and no search indexing** (Joy Q6). With personal links, the token itself acts as the access control. Still keep the site out of search engines.

## Pitfalls / complaints seen
- Exact-match name search fails on nicknames or typos and can lock guests out for hours (Zola). Name-not-found is the most common forum complaint.
- Guests unable to change an RSVP or meal choice after submitting causes frustration (WeddingWire threads).
- Data out of sync between views (website shows confirmed, app shows pending) erodes trust. Keep a single source of truth.
- Guests added to an event later are silently marked "invited" with no notification (The Knot). If the couple changes invitations after launch, the change should be visible to the guest or come with a prompt to notify.
- Fee and payment claims are inconsistent: Joy's "zero-fee" marketing conflicts with the 3.5% card fee in its help center. No product handles guests paying for their own meals or rooms. The "what you owe" view in this PRD has no model to copy, so it must be designed from scratch and the amount must be stated clearly as calculated, not paid.
- Visibility for multiple events is a source of confusion on weaker platforms (mixed Knot reports).
