### Q2 EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 (working PDF)

Working URL: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf (the `/sites/default/...` and `/sites/edpb/...` paths return 404). Landing page: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-052020-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en. All rows below: **Publisher:** EDPB | **Pub date:** 2020-05-04 (v1.1 2020-05-13) | **Accessed:** 2026-11-06 | **Confidence:** high | **Class:** regulatory.

- **Claim (a) conditionality):** Para 26: bundling consent with terms, or "tying" a service to consent for data "not necessary for the performance of that contract or service", is "highly undesirable", and such consent "is presumed to be not freely given (recital 43)". Para 32: Art. 7(4) applies only where the data are *not necessary* for the service. Para 36: "the burden of proof in Article 7(4) is on the controller". Para 39: access to services "must not be made conditional" on consent (cookie-wall example).
- **Claim (b) explicit consent):** Para 93: explicit means "the data subject must give an express statement of consent". A written, possibly signed, statement is the obvious way. Para 94: a signed statement is not required. In an online context the person can fill in an electronic form, send an email and so on. Para 96 (Example 17): a website can use an explicit consent screen "that contains Yes and No check boxes, provided that the text clearly indicates the consent, for instance 'I, hereby, consent to the processing of my data', and not for instance, 'It is clear to me that my data will be processed'". Para 98: two-stage verification (for example an email confirmation) can strengthen explicit consent.
- **Claim (c) demonstrating consent):** Para 107: the GDPR "does not prescribe exactly how", but the controller must be able to prove consent while the processing lasts. Afterwards, proof should be kept "no longer then strictly necessary" for legal obligations or legal claims. Para 108: keep a record showing "how consent was obtained, when consent was obtained and the information provided to the data subject at the time". Online, this can be session information plus "a copy of the information that was presented". Para 106: demonstrating consent should not lead to "excessive amounts of additional data processing".
- **Claim (d) withdrawal):** Para 113: withdrawal must be "as easy as giving consent and at any given time". Para 114: if consent was given through a website, the data subject "must be able to withdraw consent via the same electronic interface", "without detriment", free of charge and "without lowering service levels". Para 116: the controller must inform about the right to withdraw *before* consent is given. Para 117: earlier processing remains lawful, but the controller must stop, and if no other legal basis exists the data "should be deleted".

## Requirement implications (synthesis)

1. **Proxy answering for adults is weak as "consent".** The sources treat consent as the data subject's own act, and Helsedirektoratet states that relatives cannot consent for another adult. A requirement that one household member gives explicit Art. 9 consent *for* a spouse is therefore not well supported. Options for the PRD:
   - (a) Each adult confirms their own allergy entry, for example via their own link or a per-person consent tick that the person must confirm.
   - (b) The respondent enters the data and ticks a confirmation such as "Jeg bekrefter at de jeg svarer for er informert og har samtykket". This is a **practical mitigation, not a source-backed valid consent mechanism**. It should be flagged as a risk.
   - (c) Minimise: collect only what the kitchen needs (allergen category plus free text), and avoid diagnoses.
   - (d) For children, the parent or guardian answers and consents (holder of parental responsibility).
2. **Consent UI (EDPB):** use a separate, unticked Yes/No with wording such as "Jeg samtykker i at …" (para 96). Do not tie the RSVP to it, because RSVP is possible without dietary consent (paras 26, 32, 36). Inform about withdrawal before consent is given (para 116).
3. **Consent record:** store who consented, for whom, when, and which notice version was shown (para 108). Keep no more than needed (para 106).
4. **Withdrawal in the same interface:** the household link must offer "trekk samtykke / slett allergiopplysninger" (para 114). On withdrawal, delete the dietary data (para 117).
5. **Privacy notice page** with the Art. 13 items 1, 3, 5, 6 (hosting outside the EEA?), 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11. Items 2, 4 and 12 are likely "not applicable" but should be stated.
6. **Retention:** define a deletion date shortly after the wedding, for example once the kitchen no longer needs the data (Art. 5(1)(e)). Admin delete or export is a requirement.
7. **Hosting provider = processor:** the couple needs the provider's DPA/terms that meet Art. 28. This is a non-functional requirement to verify for the chosen hosting.
8. **Payments:** show the account number (11 digits, formatted) and a suggested "melding" (for example "Navn – middag/hytte"). Do not show a KID. Showing the couple's Vipps number or personal QR fits "private purposes". Avoid features that use Vipps "request" without prior agreement, and avoid anything that looks like a public collection. The 2027 fee does not affect one-off guest payments.
9. **Tax:** cost-sharing with no profit is not "virksomhet" under the Skatteetaten reasoning, but no source addresses this exact case. The PRD should not make tax claims and can treat this as out of scope.

## Uncertain / contested points

- Whether the household exemption (Art. 2(2)(c)) covers a private wedding site at all. Datatilsynet calls it "snevert", and online publication may fall outside it. Round 1's assumption (GDPR applies) is kept as the safe default.
- Whether a respondent's confirmation that others have consented is accepted by Datatilsynet. No source was found. Helsedirektoratet's statement is from health care, not private events.
- The Norwegian age for children's own consent outside Art. 8 information society services (Helsedirektoratet says 18 in its context; EDPB/Art. 8 says 13–16 for ISS). Not verified for this context.
- Whether showing a personal Vipps QR or phone number on a website is fine under the terms. Not prohibited and not addressed.
- The KID "business only" point was inferred, not stated by a bank help page.
- The Skatteetaten crowdfunding statement is from 2013 and does not cover reimbursement or cost-sharing.
