# D2b — Guests' voice and older users

Method note: 19 tool calls and 12 sources read in full or in part. Reddit (reddit.com) was **not reachable** with the research tool: the search API refused the domain. All forum "user voice" below therefore comes from WeddingWire forums, a single community. Under rule 3, forum findings are phrased as "seen in WeddingWire", not as prevalence. Most WeddingWire posters are couples reporting what their guests did. Few are guests speaking for themselves. Treat these as second-hand guest behaviour. Quoted search-engine summaries were not used as evidence unless the page itself was fetched. Exceptions are flagged.

## Findings

### Q1. What guests look for on a wedding website, and what they still ask about

- **Claim:** Guests keep asking the couple directly about things already on the website: location, start time, registry, accommodation, travel, hotel and food. Several couples said few guests visited the site at all. - **Claim:** "Can't find my name" in name-search RSVP is a known failure mode. ### Q3. Older adults, online forms and mobile web

- **Claim:** Most older relatives could RSVP online, but a minority could not. Many couples used a hybrid approach: paper or phone for the oldest guests, online for everyone else. Others reported failures, such as two great-aunts in their 80s who could not use the site and sent a note or an email instead. Seen in WeddingWire, about 13 distinct voices in one thread. - **Claim:** When a stamped paper card was offered alongside the website, about 80% of a younger crowd still used paper. Habit, not age, can drive the channel choice. ### Q4. Multi-day weddings and guests paying for lodging or meals

- **Claim:** Forum consensus: guests paying for their own lodging is acceptable when it is optional, priced per room and paid to the venue rather than the couple. Making a multi-night stay mandatory, or charging for meals the hosts provide, is seen as improper. Costs must be known up front, because adding a fee after saying lodging was covered is a "surprise". Seen in WeddingWire, about 7 voices in one thread. | **Verbatim:** "Otherwise it comes across as asking people to subsidize your ability to have the wedding at that venue." / "Making it an option allows the guests to decide for themselves." / "why you'd be expecting folks to stay 5 days" / "Is the venue collecting the money or you?" | **Source:** "Asking guests to pay when lodging and food is included" | **Publisher:** WeddingWire forums | **Pub date:** 2023-03-04 to 2023-03-07 | **Accessed:** 2026-11-06 | **URL:** https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/asking-guests-to-pay-when-lodging-and-food-is-included/bb7ffd0022521b15.html | **Confidence:** medium | **Class:** sentiment
- **Claim:** An etiquette columnist says guests are normally expected to pay their own accommodation. Paying the couple a large sum directly feels like buying a spot at the wedding, so payment should go through a third party, with costs flagged early. This supports the forum view from a second, independent source. | **Verbatim:** "Guests are always expected to pay for their own accommodations" / "nobody wants to feel like they have to purchase their spot at the wedding" | **Source:** "How Do We Thoughtfully Ask Guests to Pay for Their Accommodations?" | **Publisher:** A Practical Wedding | **Pub date:** 2021-03-31 | **Accessed:** 2026-11-06 | **URL:** https://apracticalwedding.com/how-do-we-thoughtfully-ask-guests-to-pay-for-their-accommodations/ | **Confidence:** medium | **Class:** sentiment
- **Note:** All of this is US-centric. Norwegian norms for multi-day weddings with paid cabin stays and meals (common at rural venues) were not researched and may differ.

### Q6. The invitation experience: digital vs paper

- **Claim:** Etiquette expert Lizzie Post (Emily Post Institute) holds that wedding invitations should still be paper, because "A mailed invitation carries clout and sentimentality". For multi-generational weddings, communication should be "clear for the most senior generation". Couples should not assume all older guests will know what to do with a digital invite, or even see it. Emailed invites get lost, and paper ones are kept as keepsakes. | **Source:** "Digital wedding invitations" | **Publisher:** Refinery29 | **Pub date:** 2017-12-19 (old; flag) | **Accessed:** 2026-11-06 | **URL:** https://www.refinery29.com/digital-wedding-invitations | **Confidence:** medium (expert opinion, not data) | **Class:** sentiment
- **Claim:** One couple used Evite for most guests and paper for a grandmother. They valued seeing who had opened the link, so they could phone those who had not. Seen in WeddingWire, 1 voice. | **Source:** "Online RSVP" | **Publisher:** WeddingWire forums | **Pub date:** 2020-01-23 | **Accessed:** 2026-11-06 | **URL:** https://www.weddingwire.com/wedding-forums/online-rsvp/227c668762244223.html | **Confidence:** low | **Class:** sentiment

## Jobs-to-be-done for guests (synthesis)

1. "When I get the invitation, I want to know immediately **if I'm invited, who in my household is included, and what I must do by when**, so I don't have to ask."
2. "When I answer for my household, I want to **find us without searching for my name**, tick each person, and **know it got through**."
3. "When plans change, I want to **change my answer easily** without phoning the couple." (Inferred; not evidenced in this round.)
4. "Before committing to a weekend, I want to know **what it costs me** (cabin, meals), **what is optional**, and **who I pay**, so there are no surprises."
5. "Close to the date, I want the **practical stuff fast**: times for each day, how to get there, parking, what to wear, what to bring. I'll reopen the link from my messages, not from the paper card."
6. "If I'm older or unsure, I want to **not be afraid of doing it wrong**, with big text and buttons, a clear way back, and a human to call."
7. "If I don't read Norwegian, I want to **read the same information in my language** without hunting for it."

## Requirement and UX implications

- **Personal link per household (no name search).** This removes the documented "can't find my name" failure. The link should land straight on the household's own invitation, listing the named people and any plus-one or children explicitly.
- **One question per screen in the RSVP**, with a progress indicator ("Steg 2 av 4"), a visible Back link, and a left-aligned "Fortsett" button (GOV.UK). Use a final "check your answers" summary before submitting.
- **Confirmation page that states what was received** ("Dere er 3 som kommer fredag–søndag"), what happens next and when, and how to change the answer. Consider an optional SMS or email copy as the "record" (GOV.UK).
- **Answers editable via the same link until a stated deadline.** The deadline should be shown on the confirmation.
- **Forgiving input and clear errors** (NN/g 65+): no date pickers or strict formats, errors placed at the field in plain language, large tap targets and base text, high contrast, and no light-grey small text.
- **Always show a human fallback**: a phone number or "send en SMS til oss" on every page. The couple must also be able to register an answer on a guest's behalf. Forums show that older guests and habitual guests will use phone, text or paper anyway.
- **Couple-side "opened / not answered" view** so they can phone non-responders (the Evite pattern). This is optional but evidenced as valued.
- **Costs up front and per item**: cabin per night or per unit, meals per day, what is optional, who is paid (the venue, not the couple, if possible), and the deadline. Show this before the RSVP asks for nights and meals, never after.
- **Practical info as a short, scannable "Praktisk" page** ordered by day: times, address and map link, parking, transport, dress code, what to bring, weather and clothing for a rural venue, and the gifts wish list. Keep the couple's story and photos separate and optional.
- **Distribute by SMS or Messenger link, not only paper.** Paper invites alone get low site traffic. Show the sender name and a recognisable domain. The link text should make clear it is from the couple, to avoid looking like smishing. Plan for re-sending the link.
- **Language switcher**: a text label ("English" / "Norsk") in a prominent top position, not flags. The household's language is preferably preset per guest link.
- **Paper or keepsake option** for the oldest guests. Etiquette sources still value paper. A printed card with the same personal link or QR code could bridge the two.
