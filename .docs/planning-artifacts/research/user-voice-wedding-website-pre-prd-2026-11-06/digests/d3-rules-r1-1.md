### A. GDPR and Norwegian privacy law

**A1. Household exemption**
- **Claim:** The GDPR does not apply to processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity and thus with no connection to a professional or commercial activity". ## Requirement implications (your synthesis — what the PRD should say)

1. **Assume GDPR applies. Do not rely on the household exemption.** The case law reads the exemption narrowly. The site is published on the internet, uses third-party hosting (which is covered by GDPR under Recital 18 in any case), and involves helpers outside the household. Treating the site as in scope is the safe and assessable choice. Document this decision and its reasoning.
2. **Treat allergies and dietary needs as health data under Art. 9.** Use explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)) as the basis for that field only. The ordinary RSVP (name, attendance, arrival, paid options) can rest on a separate, non-health basis.
3. **Consent design:**
   - Make the dietary field optional and put a separate, unticked, clearly worded consent checkbox right next to it, stating the specific purpose (passing needs to the kitchen and caterer).
   - Do not make RSVP submission depend on consent. Under Art. 7(4), consent tied to a service is presumed not freely given. Guests without consent can still RSVP and can tell the couple directly.
   - Provide a withdrawal or deletion path that is as easy as giving consent, for example via the same personal link.
   - Log when consent was given, and when it was withdrawn if it was.
4. **Data minimisation:** Use the field label "Allergier eller kosthensyn kjøkkenet må vite om". Offer predefined options plus short free text, and do not ask for diagnoses.
5. **Admin access:** Restrict access to the couple and the three named helpers. Show only the dietary info each role needs, and consider hiding it from roles that do not need it.
6. **Retention and hosting:** Delete automatically or by script three months after the wedding. Host in the EU/EEA. Have data processor agreements with the hosting and e-mail providers, who are covered by GDPR under Recital 18.
7. **Privacy notice:** The PRD should require a short Norwegian privacy notice. Art. 13 content still needs verifying (see leads). It typically covers who is responsible, the purposes, the legal bases, recipients (helpers, caterer), retention, rights, withdrawal and the right to complain to Datatilsynet.
8. **Payment:** The site shows payment information only and does not process payments.
   - Show the bank account number plus a message format such as "<household name> – bryllup".
   - Show the couple's Vipps number. Payments to the same person are free up to 5,000 NOK within 24 hours, with 1 % above that.
   - A Vipps boks QR code is an option, but it costs about 1.9 %.
   - Add an "I have paid" self-report field and a manual "registrert betalt" toggle in the admin view. Do not claim automatic reconciliation.
9. **Accessibility:** Legal UU duties likely do not apply to a private couple, which is inference. Still, adopt WCAG 2.2 AA as a self-imposed quality target, since it is also graded under criterion 4:
   - visible labels
   - 24×24 px minimum targets (larger is better for older guests)
   - 4.5:1 contrast
   - 200 % zoom and reflow at 320 px
   - visible, unobscured focus
   - inline error text plus an error summary in the Designsystemet pattern
   - no re-entry of data already known through the personal link (3.3.7)
   - personal links instead of passwords (3.3.8)

## Uncertain / contested points
- **Household exemption for an invite-only, link-protected site:** No Datatilsynet or EDPB statement was found on restricted-audience event sites. Lindqvist concerned publication to an indefinite audience, and a closed guest list is arguably different. The hosting provider is covered by GDPR either way.
- **Lindqvist and Ryneš:** Primary judgment text and paragraph numbers were not retrieved (CURIA and EUR-Lex returned empty pages). Commentary only.
- **Allergies as health data:** Allergies and dietary needs are classified as health data here by inference from the Art. 9 wording and Datatilsynet's broad reading. No source specifically on food allergies was retrieved. Pure preferences such as vegetarian are probably not health data, but religious dietary needs could reveal religious belief, which is also Art. 9 data. This needs verification.
- **Vipps 2027 fee change:** It applies from 1 March 2027, which is the wedding year. It was read only as a search excerpt, and it is unclear whether it covers only "scheduled" (faste/planlagte) payments or all private payments.
- **Vipps boks fee:** 19 NOK per 1,000 NOK (vipps.no/kasse) appears to contradict "free"-sounding wording elsewhere. The figure comes from the page as fetched.
- **"Rent private" use:** Whether collecting money for meals and accommodation counts as "private use" under Vipps terms was not tested. It plausibly does, as cost-sharing among friends and family.
